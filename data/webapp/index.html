🛰 ORBITX

A real-time satellite tracking & AI position prediction platform

ORBITX tracks 10 real satellites currently orbiting Earth — including the International Space Station, Hubble Space Telescope, NOAA weather satellites, and Landsat Earth observation satellites. It uses live orbital data from space agencies to show you exactly where each satellite is right now, and uses Machine Learning to predict where any satellite will be at a future time you choose.

✦ WHAT YOU CAN DO

🗺

Live Ground Track

See the path each satellite is flying over Earth right now, plotted on an interactive world map.

📡

Pass Predictions

Find out when a satellite will fly over your location — useful for spotting satellites with the naked eye.

🧠

AI Position Prediction

Train a machine learning model and ask it: "Where will this satellite be in 2 hours?" — then see how accurate it is.

🌍

3D Orbit View

Watch the satellite's orbit path drawn around a 3D Earth globe in your browser.

✦ HOW TO USE IT

1
Pick a satellite from the list on the left — e.g. "ISS (ZARYA)" is the International Space Station. Its dot on the map shows its current location.
2
Set your location under "Ground Station" to see when that satellite will pass over your city. Enter your city's latitude and longitude, then click "Predict Passes".
3
Use the AI Predictor — type a number of seconds into the future (e.g. 3600 = 1 hour) and click "Train & Predict". The AI will guess where the satellite will be, then compare it against the real answer.
4
Explore the tabs at the bottom: Pass Predictions, ML Prediction results, 3D Orbit, and Satellite Info.

📡 What is a Pass?
A "pass" is when a satellite flies over your location and becomes visible above your horizon. Select a satellite on the left, set your location, and click Find Passes Over Me to see the exact times it will pass over you — including how high it will appear in the sky.
🧠 How does the AI Predictor work?
The AI studies the satellite's orbital shape — things like how tilted the orbit is, how elliptical it is, and how fast the satellite moves. It then learns a mathematical pattern that maps "time in the future" → "position in space". Once trained, you can ask it "Where will this satellite be in 2 hours?" and it will give you a predicted answer, which is then compared against the true physics-calculated position.
Select a satellite and click "Train & Predict" to begin.
